# Service-bank pattern study

## Evidence and limits

Primary spatial evidence: the user-selected third alley concept, `art/reviews/xenon-001/selected-third.png`. Enlarged native-pixel views cover the left service bank at roughly x=280–520, y=0–540. No added image detail was synthesized. Published secondary reference DP-04 (Darius Puia) supports nested machinery groups, quiet large shells and repeated small elements. HM-03 supports local rhythm within dissimilar larger groups; it does not provide close-up pipe construction evidence. AF-06 is the controlled-plane reference.

This is a visual decomposition, not a claim to have recovered the artist's algorithm. Some surfaces are occluded or painterly. Pipe/plenum functions are inferred from form. Exact hidden connections, dimensions and part counts cannot be established from this image.

## Hierarchy inferred from the reference

1. **Bank:** dominant route direction, one or two important silhouettes, and space between families.
2. **Route group:** primary trunk plus subordinate companion or return, with related direction but unequal endpoints, diameter and wall offset.
3. **Motif:** equipment housing plus its neck/collar package; a bottom return; a wall offset; a terminal vent. These are coherent subassemblies.
4. **Component:** straight, bend, reducer, collar, flange, enclosure and bracket.
5. **Fine members:** bolts around a flange, repeated vent slats, a seam or lug. This level benefits from regular spacing.

The useful recursion is **group → dominant element + supporting elements + quiet space**. It stops at manufactured parts. It does not mean putting a miniature pipe bank on every pipe.

## Proposed generation rules

These are tunable starting hypotheses, not measured ratios from the artwork.

- Select route topology before adding fittings: straight, return, offset, wall entry or terminal feed.
- Give the primary route one major equipment event in a local wall patch; allow a second only when it has a different role.
- Attach flanges, necks and service panels to an event. Do not sample each part independently along the whole route.
- Use long quiet spans between events. The prototype adds native 2 m and 3 m spools instead of scaling finished kit pieces.
- Repeat close flange pairs, bolt pitches and vent slat pitches faithfully inside a motif.
- Offset neighboring equipment events vertically. Allow common structural mount datums, but avoid aligning every housing and termination.
- Choose a small number of wall-offset lanes. Let a route change lanes through an actual bend or offset component.
- Pair a return with a shorter or differently terminated companion leg; do not mirror every detail.
- Cluster related routes more closely than unrelated families. Reserve a visible quiet lane between families.
- Vary a complete motif by omission, alternate termination, leg length or position. Avoid arbitrary angle jitter.
- Use one material family per subsystem, with limited contrasting necks or covers. Avoid independent random colors per fitting.
- Prefer odd counts only when the grouping calls for them. Three equally spaced identical pipes are still repetitive.
- Stop expansion when screen-space gaps and silhouettes become unreadable. A density budget belongs to the group, not to every square meter.
- Validate port profiles, unit scale, clearance and plausible supports after assembly. A valid port join alone is not a collision check.

## What was built

A native Blender proof with a fixed workshop camera and lighting compares a flat repeated sequence with a hierarchy of routes and motifs. v001 showed excessive empty space. v002 adds a lower, secondary maintenance event and wall entry to restore density without duplicating the primary rhythm. Added master geometry: 2 m and 3 m quiet spools and a framed louver terminal.

This is a qualitative comparison, not a controlled equal-part-count experiment. It tests the plausibility of the grammar; it does not establish stylistic equivalence. The proof is not yet installed in the alley.

## Before adapting to facades

Test several distinct banks from the same bounded grammar. Judge whether they look related without sharing an obvious sequence. Compare a close view, silhouette, grayscale and actual game-view scale. Retain coherent repetition inside each group. Only then map bank/route/motif to building/bay/opening assembly.
